How I set strategy

Every brand I run starts here, and goes back around the loop once the numbers come in. Production only starts after these calls are made.

  1. Audience

    Who has to hear this, and what they already believe.

  2. Position

    The one thing this brand owns, like being the first read on Texas grid policy.

  3. Pillars

    Three or four recurring topics that every piece ladders up to.

  4. Standards

    Rules for guests, framing and tone, so the team decides the way I would.

  5. Channels

    Which format carries which message, and on what calendar.

From breaking news to published

How the strategy gets carried out when a story breaks. Rapid reactions go out within 24 to 72 hours of the news.

  1. Something breaks

    An ERCOT alert, a PUC vote, a committee hearing or a new bill.

  2. Frame it

    Decide the message: what changed, who it affects and what comes next.

  3. Capture

    Record the host or principal, or pull footage from the hearing.

  4. Cut

    Edit, caption and brand the clip, newsletter post and social copy.

  5. Publish

    Release on podcast, newsletter, YouTube, LinkedIn, X and Reddit while the news is live.

  6. Follow through

    Send guests and partners their clips, see what landed and feed it into the next one.

In practice: the SB 819 Senate hearing became eight captioned, branded testimony videos for Advanced Power Alliance's rural energy campaign, delivered 8 days after the final brief.

Writing sample

A short public explainer on a Railroad Commission issue, written the way I'd write for an office.

Spec writing sample by Nate Peavey. Not written for, approved by or affiliated with Commissioner-elect Rosenthal or the Railroad Commission.

Texas has more abandoned oil wells than ever. Here's why that matters.

An orphan well is an oil or gas well whose operator went broke or walked away without plugging it. Left open, it can leak gas, and old wells can blow out. One near Toyah in West Texas spewed a foul plume for 19 days.

Texas had 10,029 of them in August 2025, up 1,682 in a single year. The state plugged 775 with its own money that year, 225 short of its target. By the end of 2025 the list passed 11,000, a 20-year high.

When an operator can't pay, taxpayers do. Commissioner-elect Jon Rosenthal has said he wants to speed up plugging and raise the bonds drillers post for new permits. Bigger bonds mean operators, not taxpayers, cover more of the cleanup.

Sources: Railroad Commission FY 2025 cleanup report, Houston Chronicle, Texas Tribune, S&P Global.
